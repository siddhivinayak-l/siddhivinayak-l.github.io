About

Understanding memory through the movement of ions.

I am a PhD Research Scholar and Dean’s Doctoral Fellow at the University of Manchester. My doctoral work explores ionic memory in Ångström-scale two-dimensional channels.

I study how confinement, interfaces and capacitance shape a device’s electrical response. Alongside these experiments, I develop Python measurement tools and apply machine learning to electrochemical data.

At the bench

Experimental work

Ion transport, impedance spectroscopy, transient measurements and pulse-based characterisation.

Computational work

Machine learning, automated instrument control and reproducible data analysis.

Brain-inspired computing

I apply machine learning to measurements from perovskites, batteries and sensors. These models support parameter estimation, impedance interpretation and more informed experimental choices.

How I work

Connecting signals to physical mechanisms.

Careful measurement, physical interpretation and repeatable experiments.

Measure

Electrical and electrochemical protocols capture the time, voltage and frequency dependence of ionic devices.

Interpret

Impedance and transient analysis help distinguish intrinsic memory from interfacial and electrode effects.

Automate

Python control and standardised analysis make protocols repeatable and prepare experiments for model-guided decisions.
